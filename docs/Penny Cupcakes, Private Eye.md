Penny Cupcakes.. She was once the star of the show—face painted, balloons twisted, laughter on command... But the years of pratfalls and honking noses wore thin. Kids just weren't laughing like they used to. And the kids' parents? Well.. of course, they were uneasy. They'd be uneasy at the site of -any- clown. In today's world, clowns weren't the "good guys" anymore. It is what it is. No rewriting history there...

So, about 10 years ago, she *officially* hung up the clown suit.

Nowadays, she scrapes by as a PI - trading slapstick for stakeouts and the circus tent for the city’s underbelly. She lurks streets slick with the night's rain, gazes into neon reflections dancing in muddy puddles, and breathes air carrying the lingering scent of diesel fumes and cheap cigars as the waits for her client's husband to exit shady motels with his mistress. It's the only thing that lets her believe she's still making a difference, even if it's in the shadows.

Penny steps heavy-footed, walks with a squeak. Her suit’s well-fitting, which stands in contrast to the bright nose and oversized shoes - old habits die slowly. And the helium addiction? A leftover from the act—what started as a gag turned into a crutch. Sure, squeaky-voiced interrogations can be a bit distracting, but that's a sacrifice she's willing to make.

Turns out, she's actually good at her job and, despite the air of silliness that her physical presence still carries, behind the (mostly) trim attire, she was born for this job.. even if she doesn't know it yet. Because, underneath it all, they’re sharp, observant—years of studying audience reactions, catching the subtlest shifts in expression, knowing when to draw attention and when to vanish behind the curtains. She reads people like an old routine, practiced and precise. And when the case is on, the clowning stops.

Almost.
